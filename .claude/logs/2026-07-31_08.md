## Item 5 — the interactive what-if layer

Scoped first, which surfaced the question worth asking: a client-side recompute needs `computePlayoffPicture` to run in the browser. Checked rather than assumed — `playoff-picture.ts`'s only external imports are `import type`, and its one internal import (`./dashboard`) is likewise type-only at the external boundary. So the module is genuinely browser-safe and both options were real.

Clarify (3 questions, all answered with the recommended option):

1. **Client-side, instant.** The remaining schedule ships with the page and a toggle re-runs the calculation in the browser. This is the section's first and only client module.
2. **Banner + changed rows marked.** A persistent hypothetical banner with a reset, plus a visible mark on every team whose status differs from reality. This is the mitigation for the layer's one real risk — a what-if "Eliminated" read as settled fact by someone who scrolled past the banner.
3. **Explain and hide when nothing remains.** The connected 2025 league is season-complete, so it has zero remaining games; the toggles are replaced by a line saying so and naming the full-season matchup sweep, rather than a greyed-out empty rail.

Two pure additions to the service, both covered by their own tests:

- `remainingGames` — **fully unplayed pairs only.** A half-scored matchup (one side in, one pending) is a sync artifact rather than an upcoming game, and offering a toggle on it would invite the user to overwrite a result that already happened.
- `applyHypotheticalResults(rows, winners)` — non-mutating, and the entire interactive layer's logic. Deciding a game turns it from remaining into played; `computePlayoffPicture` then produces the hypothetical picture through the identical deterministic bounding. **This is what makes "no new probabilistic logic" structurally true rather than merely observed:** there is one status function in the codebase, and a probability could not enter the what-if view without entering the real one.

**A hypothetical moves a record, never points-for.** The synthetic 1–0 is a result, not a score; `isHypothetical` keeps it out of `pointsFor`, so a seed decided on the points tiebreaker still turns on the real season. Disclosed in the banner rather than left to be inferred from a column that did not move — inventing a plausible score would put a fabricated number in a column the rest of the app reports as fact.

Guard worth naming: a winner who is not in the game they were named for is IGNORED, not applied. The naive per-row implementation would have set both sides to 0 and silently produced a tie nobody asked for. Fixed by collecting each unplayed game's participants first; asserted by a test.

## Item 6 — the property, asserted rather than trusted

"Explicitly avoid full scenario trees or any visual resembling a probability distribution/simulation output" is not a build step, so it is enforced mechanically over the rendered markup, in BOTH the resting and hypothetical views: no percentage, no `probab|likelihood|odds|chance|simulat` vocabulary, no `<meter>`/`<progress>`/`role="progressbar"`, and no width-as-value geometry (the shape a probability display takes even when it is never labelled one). Those assertions ARE the item and they run on every commit. There is also no branching scenario tree: toggles are a flat per-game list, never a tree of outcomes.

Caught by its own test: the shell's original disclaimer read "Nothing here is simulated or weighted by likelihood" and tripped the vocabulary assertion. Reworded rather than exempted — an exemption would have made the assertion decorative.
